The Utah Files · Part II

What Their Own Experts Admit

Addiction. Withdrawal. Liver injury. Drug interactions. Neurological effects. The plaintiffs' experts defend Feel Free — but the evidence they submitted to federal court does not describe kratom as risk-free.

Case No. 2:26-cv-00267-HCN-DBP · U.S. District Court, District of Utah
AddictionAcknowledged in BT-submitted expert evidence
WithdrawalRecognized in the scientific literature
Liver + KidneyToxicity concerns reviewed by BT's expert
SeizuresAmong the neurological risks discussed

The Short Version

10-Second Summary

  • Botanic Tonics submitted expert reports arguing that Feel Free, when used exactly as labeled, is not shown to be unsafe.
  • Those same materials acknowledge adverse effects associated with kratom, especially at higher doses, with chronic use, abuse, adulteration, or susceptible users.
  • The risks discussed include addiction, withdrawal, seizures, psychosis, liver injury, kidney injury, cardiovascular effects, respiratory effects, overdose and death.
  • One BT expert attempted to calculate a Lowest Observable Adverse Effect Level (LOAEL) and a proposed upper safe mitragynine dose.

BT's experts do not say kratom is harmless. Their defense is narrower: harms reported with kratom generally do not automatically prove that Feel Free, at its labeled composition and dose, causes those same harms.

BT's Own Expert Lists the Adverse Effects

Robert Verkerk, PhD, prepared a report specifically addressing generic kratom, kava, and Botanic Tonics' Feel Free products. In his section on kratom toxicity, he says long-term and high-dose use has been associated with:

Neurological & Behavioral

  • Anxiety
  • Irritability
  • Aggression
  • Tremors
  • Seizures
  • Psychosis
  • Cognitive impairment
  • Dizziness and drowsiness
  • Coma

Physical & Organ Effects

  • Anorexia and weight loss
  • Skin hyperpigmentation
  • Respiratory distress
  • Liver injury / hepatotoxicity
  • Kidney injury / nephrotoxicity
  • Impaired lung function
  • Severe primary hypothyroidism
These are not adverse effects supplied by Utah regulators or outside critics. They appear in a scientific report submitted in support of Botanic Tonics.

Addiction, Dependence and Withdrawal

Verkerk's report says evidence of kratom addiction has become “increasingly apparent” as non-traditional use has expanded outside Southeast Asia.

  • Addiction
  • Dependence
  • Withdrawal symptoms
  • Drug interactions, particularly with medications metabolized by the liver

He also identifies 7-hydroxymitragynine as an important contributor to addictive potential and states that high-dose, chronic mitragynine exposure can increase 7-OH formation and therefore increase addiction risk.

The dispute is not whether addiction and withdrawal exist in the literature. The dispute is how strongly those risks apply to a particular product, dose and pattern of use.

The FDA Toxicity List Appears Inside BT's Expert Report

Verkerk reproduces FDA's description of health effects associated with kratom toxicity:

Neurologic / Psychiatric

  • Nervousness
  • Agitation
  • Aggression
  • Sleeplessness
  • Hallucinations
  • Delusions
  • Tremors

Physical

  • Respiratory depression
  • Constipation
  • Nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Loss of libido
  • Skin hyperpigmentation
  • Severe withdrawal signs and symptoms

Verkerk argues these symptoms are infrequent or absent at lower exposures resembling traditional use. But they remain part of the safety analysis he submitted.

Liver and Kidney Injury

Verkerk identifies liver damage as a documented risk and says high doses may impair kidney function. His review also describes animal findings involving elevated liver enzymes, elevated creatinine, and impaired kidney and lung function.

The careful takeaway is not that every kratom user will suffer organ injury. It is that liver and kidney toxicity are among the adverse outcomes recognized in BT's own supporting scientific materials.

Cardiovascular and Respiratory Effects

In summarizing the EFSA risk review, Verkerk lists human reports involving:

  • Palpitations
  • Tachycardia
  • Cardiac arrest
  • Respiratory problems
  • Death

The expert materials stress uncertainty over causation in individual deaths and the importance of dose, other drugs and product composition. That is different from saying overdose or mortality risk does not exist.

Seizures, Psychosis and Cognitive Effects

Verkerk's summary identifies seizures and psychosis as significant neurological concerns, particularly with long-term use. His review also discusses cognitive impairment in animal evidence and neurological effects in human reports.

Oliver Grundmann, PhD, whose statement was submitted by Botanic Tonics, likewise acknowledges that kratom and kava affect the central nervous system and may affect cognition and response time at high doses.

A Proposed Toxicity Threshold From BT's Expert

Verkerk attempts to derive actual safety thresholds from traditional-use data.

Verkerk's assessmentAmount
Lowest Observable Adverse Effect Level (LOAEL) he derives from traditional-use data8 g dry leaf equivalent/day
Proposed upper safe mitragynine level20 mg per dose
Suggested spacingAt least 6 hours
Suggested maximumNo more than 2 doses in 24 hours

He reaches the 20 mg figure using assumptions about traditional exposure, mitragynine content and an additional uncertainty factor because the dataset is incomplete.

The same report arguing for a safe labeled exposure also acknowledges that robust high-quality evidence remains limited and that universal safety thresholds are difficult to establish.

Who Does BT's Expert Say Should Be Warned?

Verkerk recommends precautions for sensitive groups, including:

  • Patients with liver disease
  • Patients with neurologic disease
  • Patients with pulmonary disease
  • Patients with renal disease
  • Patients with cardiac disease
  • Pregnant women
  • People with a history of substance or polysubstance abuse

What Oliver Grundmann Acknowledges

  • Historical hepatotoxicity concerns involving kava preparations
  • Potential adverse effects in the underlying literature
  • Effects on the central nervous system
  • Potential effects on cognition and response time at high doses
  • Dermopathy after very heavy chronic kava exposure

Grundmann ultimately concludes that Feel Free, if used according to its label, does not constitute a substantial risk. That conclusion should be reported alongside the risk concessions in the underlying discussion.

The Grundmann–Henningfield Paper Also Lists Adverse Effects

A 2024 paper coauthored by Grundmann and submitted in the litigation describes these commonly reported adverse effects:

Commonly Reported

  • Indigestion
  • Nausea
  • Dizziness
  • Constipation
  • Heart palpitations

Other Risks Discussed

  • Kratom use disorder
  • Withdrawal
  • Potential hepatic toxicity
  • Seizures
  • Sedative-like effects at high doses

The authors argue that many effects are generally tolerable or uncommon and that kratom's overdose profile differs from conventional opioids. They do not claim kratom is devoid of adverse effects or can never contribute to death.

What This Evidence Actually Establishes

  • It does show that BT's own supporting scientists acknowledge a broad toxicological profile for kratom.
  • It does show that addiction, dependence and withdrawal are recognized phenomena in the literature they relied upon.
  • It does show that liver, kidney, neurological, cardiovascular and respiratory risks appear in their own safety discussion.
  • It does show that one BT expert proposed a LOAEL and a specific upper mitragynine dose.
  • It does not prove that every kratom product causes every listed effect.
  • It does not prove that every reported death or serious adverse event was caused by kratom alone.
  • It does not establish that Feel Free, used exactly according to its label, causes all of these harms.
The accurate takeaway is narrower — and stronger: Botanic Tonics' defense is not that kratom has no adverse effects. It is that the known adverse effects should not automatically be attributed to its particular product at its labeled dose.
