What a 2026 Human Study Reveals About Kratom Abuse Potential

Coe, Huestis and colleagues | Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical research

2026 HUMAN CLINICAL STUDY · RESEARCH SUMMARY

At a glance

Controlled human research found that dried kratom leaf can produce measurable, dose-related subjective drug effects. The highest single dose differed significantly from placebo on the study’s drug-effect measures. Abuse-related adverse events were more common at higher doses, although investigators described the overall effects as modest.

No serious adverse events or clinically meaningful opioid withdrawal were observed in this short trial. Those findings are important, but do not establish long-term safety or rule out dependence after prolonged use.

Concerning effects and findings

These are effects and observations reported in the trial, not proof that every participant experienced them or that they indicate addiction.

What the researchers tested

Design

Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in healthy kratom-naive adults.

Exposure

Dried leaf powder containing 6.65–53.2 mg mitragynine; single-dose and repeated-dose conditions.

Drug effects

Drug Effects Questionnaire (DEQ) and reports of abuse-related treatment-emergent adverse events.

Withdrawal

Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale (COWS) and Subjective Opioid Withdrawal Scale (SOWS).

Four findings worth examining

01

Dried leaf produced subjective drug effects

Responses generally rose with dose. Statistically significant differences from placebo were observed only at the highest single dose on the specified DEQ measures.

02

Abuse-related events increased with dose

The pattern supports further examination of abuse liability. An adverse-event category is not itself proof of compulsive drug use.

03

Repeated dosing did not settle tolerance questions

Some subjective scores declined during repeated dosing. Three participants with relatively high responses withdrew before completing that phase, complicating interpretation.

04

No clinically meaningful withdrawal in this protocol

That is a genuine negative finding. Fifteen days in selected volunteers cannot answer what happens after months or years of heavier use.

Why this matters for Schedule I review

Evidence supporting further scheduling scrutiny

Controlled human evidence of dose-associated psychoactive effects and abuse-related events is relevant to assessing abuse potential.

What cannot be concluded

The authors characterized the effects as modest. This study alone does not establish high abuse potential, addiction rates, or the complete legal case for Schedule I.

What other evidence is needed

Long-term dependence, withdrawal, patterns of misuse, population harms, toxicology, and accepted medical-use evidence must be evaluated separately.

Important distinction

The results concern a standardized dried-leaf preparation, not every commercial extract or concentrated kratom-derived compound.

What the study does not show

  • No serious adverse events or deaths were reported in this trial.
  • No clinically meaningful opioid withdrawal was detected using the study’s criteria.
  • The study was short and used healthy, kratom-naive volunteers.
  • Results cannot establish the safety of chronic, high-frequency, or unregulated commercial use.

Source: Coe M, Huestis M, Wang D, et al. Assessment of Abuse Potential-Related Effects of Oral Dried Kratom Leaf Powder in Healthy Normal Participants Following Single and Multiple Daily Doses. DOI: 10.1002/dta.70089 ↗
