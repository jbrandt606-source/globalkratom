The Gas Station Pipeline

Inside the 2026 NACS Show and the companies bringing kratom marketing to America’s convenience-store industry

Inside the 2026 NACS Show

The National Association of Convenience Stores (NACS) will hold its 2026 convention October 6–9 in Las Vegas, with the expo running October 7–9. NACS describes the show as a place where convenience retailers discover suppliers, products and services for their stores.

Among the scheduled exhibitors are companies connected to kratom products, including VIVAZEN, Botanic Tonics, Kanva Botanicals and Diversified Botanics.

That would be notable on its own. NACS's own rules make it more significant.

NACS's Own Kratom Rules

NACS defines “kratom products” broadly to include products containing kratom or its alkaloids, including mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), whether naturally or synthetically derived.

NACS also tells attendees that FDA states kratom is not lawfully marketed in the United States as a drug product, dietary supplement, or food additive in conventional food.

What Are They Actually Promoting?

Being listed as an exhibitor does not by itself establish which specific products a company will promote at the show. That distinction matters.

NACS says it does not independently evaluate the legality of products shown at the exposition. Exhibitors instead warrant compliance with applicable federal, state and local law, and NACS says an illegal or unlawfully marketed exhibited product will be removed if NACS becomes aware of it.

For kratom, the physical product cannot be sold, distributed or openly possessed at the show—but promotional materials, labels and empty packaging are permitted.

That raises a straightforward question: what products, formulations and claims will these exhibitors actually present to convenience-store buyers?

From Manufacturer to Gas Station Shelf

MANUFACTURER→TRADE SHOW→RETAIL BUYER→CONVENIENCE STORE→CONSUMER

Trade shows are one point in the commercial chain connecting manufacturers and suppliers with the businesses that decide what appears on retail shelves. NACS requires a proposed exhibit to qualify as a legitimate product or service sold to or through convenience stores.

The 2026 show therefore offers a useful window into how kratom-connected businesses seek access to the convenience-store marketplace—even while the show itself imposes special restrictions on kratom products.

Why the Regulatory Question Matters

FDA continues to maintain Import Alert 54-15 for dietary supplements and bulk dietary ingredients that are or contain Mitragyna speciosa or kratom. FDA has also warned consumers about concentrated 7-OH products and states that kratom is not lawfully marketed as a dietary supplement or conventional-food ingredient.

This does not mean every promotional display by a kratom-connected company is necessarily unlawful. It does mean that retailers and trade-show organizers have reason to ask exactly what is being promoted, what it contains, how it is labeled and on what legal basis it is being sold.

Sources: FDA Import Alert 54-15 · FDA 7-OH Consumer Warning
