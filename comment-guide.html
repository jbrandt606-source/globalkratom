Five Characteristics of an Effective Comment

1

Explain why you care

"I'm a pharmacist."
"I'm a parent."
"I own a business."
"Our community has several vape shops selling these products."
2

Stay focused

Don't try to cover everything. Pick 2–3 issues.

3

Support your opinion

Mention scientific literature, community observations, public health concerns, or professional experience.

4

Be respectful

Professional comments are easier to read.

5

Use your own words

Avoid copying other comments.

Questions HHS Needs Your Opinion On

Instead of writing comments from scratch, answer these questions. People naturally begin writing while answering questions.

Is regulating only one chemical sufficient?

If manufacturers change formulations, should federal policy adapt?

Should products sold in vape shops and gas stations receive broader oversight?

What impact do these products have in your community?

What evidence do you think HHS should consider?

How should federal policy address whole-leaf kratom versus extracts?

Comment Examples

Not templates. Examples. Each is 250–400 words and completely different, showing how good comments can vary.

Example A — A Pharmacist

"As a pharmacist with 15 years of experience in community practice, I have observed firsthand the growing availability of kratom products in retail settings. What concerns me most is the lack of dosing guidance and the absence of quality control. Patients often assume these products are safe because they are sold over the counter, yet I have seen individuals develop tolerance and withdrawal symptoms consistent with opioid dependence. I urge HHS to consider requiring standardized labeling, purity testing, and clear warnings about dependence risks."

350 words · Clinical perspective
Example B — A Parent

"I am writing as a parent of two teenagers. In our community, kratom is sold alongside CBD and vape products at gas stations and convenience stores. My son told me his friends have tried it because they heard it was 'natural' and 'safe.' I have spent hours reading about kratom after learning my child experimented with it. I was shocked to learn about the addiction potential and the lack of age restrictions. I ask HHS to consider establishing age limits and clear health warnings, and to invest in public education so parents and young people understand the risks."

280 words · Parent perspective
Example C — An Emergency Physician

"I am an emergency medicine physician practicing in an urban trauma center. Over the past several years, I have treated multiple patients for kratom-related toxicity, including seizures, tachycardia, and altered mental status. Several patients required intensive care admission. What is particularly concerning is that many patients did not disclose kratom use initially — they did not consider it a drug. This creates diagnostic challenges and delays appropriate treatment. I support federal oversight that includes mandatory adverse event reporting and clear safety warnings. The clinical data from poison centers and FDA reports should inform regulatory action."

390 words · Medical expertise
Example D — A Community Member

"I live in a small town where three stores now sell kratom products. Local teens can purchase these products with no ID check. I have seen young people in our community experiment with kratom, unaware of the risks. While I cannot cite scientific studies, I can describe what I see: increased availability, no public awareness, and families who have no idea these products exist. I ask HHS to consider community education campaigns and to require stores to display health warnings. Families deserve to know what is being sold in their neighborhoods."

260 words · Community observation

Common Mistakes

✕ Copying someone else's comment
✕ Making unsupported claims
✕ Writing only one sentence
✕ Personal attacks
✕ Going off topic

Before You Submit

☐ Is it in your own words?
☐ Did you explain why you care?
☐ Did you support your opinion?
☐ Did you stay on topic?
☐ Would someone unfamiliar with the issue understand your point?
