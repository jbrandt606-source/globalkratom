Kratom vs. 7-OH vs. Mitragynine Pseudoindoxyl

All can produce serious toxicity. They do not necessarily produce it in the same way.

Clinical evidence comparison

Three related substances. Different toxicity profiles.

The kratom debate is increasingly framed as a choice between “natural kratom” and dangerous concentrated or chemically modified derivatives. Human clinical evidence does not support that simple division.

Kratom and mitragynine have the longest human exposure record and the largest published human case literature here, including neurologic, hepatic, renal, muscular and cardiac adverse events. Concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) is producing a newer, more recognizably opioid-like pattern that includes profound central nervous system depression, dependence and withdrawal. Mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MP) is newer still; human literature is sparse, but severe opioid-like withdrawal has already been published.

The question is not which molecule wins a contest for “safest.” The question is what each molecule actually does in the human body.

Case reports and case series establish clinical observations after reported exposure; they do not by themselves establish incidence or causation. Product composition, co-ingestants and toxicologic confirmation vary substantially. Systematic reviews can summarize those reports but do not remove the limitations of the underlying evidence.

How much human 7-OH literature is now in PubMed?

A targeted PubMed search conducted October 4, 2026 identified at least 11 human clinical case-report or case-series publications specifically involving concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). Ten describe individual patients and one retrospective series describes nine patients, for at least 19 described patients. This count excludes animal studies, mechanistic papers, reviews and poison-center surveillance from the case-report total.

FDA reports are signals, not causation findings. “Died” means death was recorded as an outcome in the report; it does not establish that FDA determined the suspect product caused the death. Spontaneous reports are affected by incomplete information, concomitant drugs, duplicates and reporting bias.

Poison Centers show how quickly 7-OH is changing the picture

America's Poison Centers reported 3,803 kratom or kratom-derivative exposures in 2025 and 3,190 through June 30, 2026. Among single-substance kratom/derivative exposures, 44.5% had serious health problems and 25.9% were hospitalized.

For 7-OH specifically, Poison Centers recorded 593 exposures in 2025 and 901 in only the first six months of 2026. Among 7-OH-only exposures, 38.8% had serious health problems and 20.5% were hospitalized.

Thebaine: nature is not a toxicology classification

The opium poppy provides a useful warning against treating “natural” and “synthetic” as synonyms for safe and dangerous.

Thebaine is a naturally occurring opium-poppy alkaloid. In a cluster of 40 human poisonings from thebaine-contaminated poppy seeds, analytically confirmed cases developed muscle spasms, rigidity and convulsions. Five developed rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney injury and metabolic acidosis; two suffered cardiac arrest and one died.

Thebaine is also an important pharmaceutical starting material. Chemical transformation can produce hydrocodone. Hydrocodone does not share thebaine's characteristic strychnine-like neuromuscular toxicity profile; it is a clinically useful opioid whose principal life-threatening overdose hazard is opioid-induced respiratory depression.

Related molecules do not have to poison people in the same way.

Thebaine can produce convulsions, rhabdomyolysis and cardiac arrest. Hydrocodone's defining overdose danger is respiratory depression. Likewise, evidence of opioid-like toxicity from 7-OH or MP does not erase the independent neurologic, hepatic, cardiac and muscular toxicity reported with kratom.

Nature is a source of molecules. It is not a certificate of safety.
