Dependence, Tolerance & Withdrawal

Clinical evidence of opioid-like dependence, withdrawal syndromes, ICU-level care, and medication-assisted treatment associated with kratom use.

PharmacologyDependenceHospital casesTreatment evidenceSurveillanceEvidence limits

Key findings

  • Published human reports document physical dependence, tolerance, opioid-like withdrawal and cases meeting substance use disorder criteria.
  • Reported withdrawal symptoms include anxiety, agitation, insomnia, gastrointestinal distress, muscle pain and autonomic symptoms.
  • Severe presentations have involved intensive care; some reports include concurrent illness or medication exposure.
  • Opioid antagonists such as naltrexone can precipitate withdrawal in physically dependent users.
  • Published cases describe use of buprenorphine and other medications to manage withdrawal or support cessation.

Dependence, tolerance and withdrawal

Physical dependence is adaptation to repeated exposure; tolerance is a reduced response to a given dose. Neither alone establishes a substance use disorder, which requires a broader pattern of impairment or distress.

Repeated exposure to kratom alkaloids
↓
Opioid receptor activity and physiological adaptation
↓
Possible tolerance and physical dependence
↓
Reduction, cessation or opioid receptor blockade
↓
Possible withdrawal symptoms

Conceptual pathway, not an inevitable progression for every user. Product composition, exposure duration and individual susceptibility vary.
