Dependence, Tolerance & Withdrawal

Clinical evidence of opioid-like dependence, withdrawal syndromes, ICU-level care, and medication-assisted treatment associated with kratom use.

Key Findings

  • Kratom produces opioid-like physical dependence and withdrawal, meeting DSM‑5 criteria for substance use disorder.
  • Withdrawal symptoms include anxiety, agitation, insomnia, abdominal pain, diarrhea, myalgias, and autonomic instability.
  • Severe withdrawal has required ICU admission, high‑dose benzodiazepines, and vasopressors.
  • Medication‑assisted treatment (buprenorphine/naloxone, clonidine) has been used successfully to manage withdrawal and support cessation.
  • Precipitated withdrawal can occur when naltrexone is given to kratom‑dependent individuals.

Clinical Withdrawal Management Summary

Published case reports and poison center surveillance demonstrate that kratom produces opioid‑type physical dependence and withdrawal. Symptoms include anxiety, agitation, insomnia, abdominal pain, diarrhea, myalgias, rhinorrhea, and autonomic instability (tachycardia, hypertension). Severe withdrawal has required ICU admission, high‑dose benzodiazepines, and vasopressors. Precipitated withdrawal can occur when naltrexone is administered to a kratom‑dependent individual.

Medication‑assisted treatment options successfully used include:

  • Buprenorphine/naloxone (induction and maintenance)
  • Clonidine (alpha‑2 agonist) for symptom control
  • Hydroxyzine, benzodiazepines for agitation/anxiety
  • Clomipramine for protracted withdrawal psychiatric symptoms

The need for MAT to discontinue kratom use in many cases underscores its addiction potential and supports consideration of scheduling under the Controlled Substances Act.
