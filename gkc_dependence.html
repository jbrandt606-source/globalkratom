Whole-Leaf Kratom Dependence Cannot Be Ignored

Connecticut consumer accounts reveal severe withdrawal, daily dependence, and functional reliance on ordinary kratom leaf.

Analysis · Global Kratom Policy Review

A Connecticut kratom advocacy statement was intended to show why "natural leaf" kratom should remain legal. Instead, several of its own consumer accounts raise serious concerns about long-term dependence, withdrawal, and an inability to function without continued kratom use.

Importantly, these accounts describe whole-leaf kratom, not concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine tablets or synthetic derivatives. The products are repeatedly identified as "pure leaf," "raw leaf," "natural kratom powder," "organic," and "unenhanced." Although the statement provides no laboratory results, these were most likely conventional leaf products containing less than the DEA's proposed 0.05% 7-OH threshold. That means the dependence described cannot simply be blamed on newer, high-potency 7-OH products.

Daily Use and Severe Withdrawal

The clearest admission comes from a Connecticut resident who began using kratom in 2014:

"I have used it daily since then."
— Connecticut resident, KCAC statement

She then states:

"I do have pretty severe withdrawals from kratom."
— Same resident, KCAC statement

She says she intends to keep taking it for as long as possible because she is worried about what she might do without it. This is direct evidence of prolonged daily use, physical withdrawal, and fear of discontinuation involving ordinary kratom leaf.

Another consumer reports tapering daily doses to make the remaining supply last longer. Others describe the loss of access as "devastating," say they can no longer function normally, or explain that they relocated to another state to maintain access.

These accounts do not prove that every kratom consumer has a substance use disorder. They do, however, document warning signs that regulators cannot dismiss:

  • Years of daily use
  • Severe withdrawal
  • Difficulty tapering
  • Anxiety about running out
  • Continued use despite legal consequences
  • A belief that ordinary functioning is impossible without the substance

Dependence Is Not Limited to Enhanced 7-OH

The statement attempts to separate whole-leaf kratom from concentrated 7-OH products. That distinction may be relevant to potency, but it does not eliminate the dependence concerns described by the consumers themselves.

One recovery specialist quoted in the document acknowledges that some people used kratom to stop opioids but later could not stop kratom:

"Some people were not able to taper off kratom."
— Recovery specialist, KCAC statement

That is an important admission. Replacing heroin or prescription opioids with kratom may reduce certain immediate risks, but substitution is not the same as recovery. Some users may simply exchange one form of opioid dependence for another.

Withdrawal Can Be Mistaken for Proof That Kratom Is Necessary

Several consumers describe worsening pain, sleeplessness, fatigue, anxiety, or loss of motivation after stopping kratom. Those symptoms may reflect the return of an underlying condition. But after years of repeated use, they may also reflect withdrawal, rebound symptoms, or both.

The advocacy statement assumes that feeling worse after stopping proves kratom was successfully treating a medical condition. It does not consider that prolonged exposure may have contributed to the distress experienced after discontinuation.

That uncertainty is precisely why an unapproved, opioid-active product should not be sold as though it were an ordinary supplement.

Why Whole-Leaf Kratom Should Be Scheduled

The Connecticut testimonies support several conclusions:

  • Whole-leaf kratom can be used daily for many years.
  • Some consumers report severe withdrawal and difficulty tapering.
  • Others describe profound psychological and functional reliance.
  • The product is being used without FDA approval or standardized medical supervision for chronic pain, opioid addiction, anxiety, depression, ADHD, fibromyalgia, fatigue, and sleep problems.

The statement itself acknowledges that no kratom-derived product has been FDA-approved for any medical indication and that additional controlled clinical trials are needed.

Scheduling only products above a 0.05% 7-OH threshold would therefore address only part of the problem. These accounts involve ordinary leaf products that were most likely below that limit, yet they still describe daily use, withdrawal, failed tapering, and an inability to function without continued access.

The Leaf Is Part of the Problem

The people quoted in this statement deserve compassion, appropriate pain care, and medically supervised treatment. But compassion should not require ignoring what their stories reveal.

The strongest evidence of dependence often comes from those who believe they cannot live normally without the drug.

These Connecticut accounts show that kratom dependence is not confined to concentrated or synthetic 7-OH. It can occur with whole-leaf kratom itself. That evidence supports treating the botanical product as a serious opioid-active substance and placing it in Schedule I, rather than creating a regulatory loophole for products below the proposed 0.05% 7-OH threshold.
