Botanic Tonics Investigation: FDA Safety Claim Under Senate Scrutiny

Senate Finance Committee probes industry flagship brand over allegedly false marketing statements

You Would Think an Industry on Life Support Would Want to Put Its Best Foot Forward…

…but nooooooo.

Instead, Botanic Tonics decided to light a match in a room full of gasoline.

At the very moment the kratom industry is facing federal scheduling, state bans, lawsuits, mounting overdose data, FDA warnings, and increasing scrutiny from Congress, the industry's flagship brand somehow thought this was the perfect time to tell consumers:

“FDA reinforces safety of natural leaf kratom.”

There was just one small problem.

The FDA never said that.

Not even close.

According to the August 3 letter from Senate Finance Committee Ranking Member Ron Wyden to Botanic Tonics CEO Jerry Ross, the banner appeared prominently on the Feel Free website despite the FDA continuing to state exactly the opposite—that kratom is not appropriate as a dietary supplement, that there are no legally marketed FDA-approved kratom products, and that consumers are warned against using kratom because of risks including liver toxicity, seizures, and substance use disorder.

That's not marketing.

That's hanging a fake diploma on the wall and hoping nobody can read.

The Letter Gets Worse

Wyden isn't simply asking why the banner existed.

He's opening a Senate Finance Committee investigation into Botanic Tonics, its relationships with senior federal officials, and the broader public health implications surrounding the industry.

The now-removed “FDA reinforces safety” banner on Feel Free
Botanic Tonics CEO Jerry Ross – now under Senate scrutiny

Then comes what may be the most uncomfortable part.

After the DEA announced action against concentrated 7-OH products, the company quietly removed the “FDA reinforces safety” banner…

…and replaced it with another one.

Now consumers were told that natural kratom has “centuries of safe use” and that synthetic 7-OH products “bear absolutely no resemblance” to natural leaf products.

Wyden calls that false as well.

Apparently when one questionable marketing claim catches fire, the solution is to pour another gallon on it.

This Isn't Just Embarrassing for Feel Free

This lands on the entire industry.

For years, kratom lobbyists have insisted they are responsible stakeholders who simply want reasonable regulation.

Then one of the industry's biggest brands publishes statements a United States Senator characterizes as false and misleading, while simultaneously becoming the subject of a congressional investigation.

That is not exactly the polished image you bring to Capitol Hill.

It's the legislative equivalent of showing up to court wearing a ski mask.

Then the Questions Start…

The letter doesn't merely criticize.

It demands answers.

Among them:

  • Why was the FDA safety banner posted?
  • Why was it removed?
  • Why replace it with another allegedly false claim?
  • When did Feel Free decide to add warnings that its own products “can become habit-forming and cause serious adverse health effects”?
  • What adverse health effects does the company believe its products cause?
  • What research supports its positions?
  • What communications has it had with senior government officials?
  • What political contributions has the company and its executives made?
  • What lobbying has occurred regarding kratom policy?

That's not a friendly request for clarification.

That's seventeen pages' worth of “Start explaining.”

The Industry Keeps Creating Its Own Problems

The irony is almost impossible to ignore.

Kratom advocates frequently argue that criticism comes from misunderstanding.

Then one of the industry's largest companies hands critics exactly what they could have wished for:

  • marketing that allegedly contradicts the FDA,
  • shifting website narratives,
  • congressional scrutiny,
  • and questions about lobbying and political influence.

You couldn't script a worse public relations strategy if you tried.

Final Thought

Industries under existential threat usually become more disciplined.

More careful.

More transparent.

Instead, Botanic Tonics appears to have handed Congress a ready-made exhibit.

If you're trying to convince lawmakers that your industry deserves their trust, telling consumers the FDA has “reinforced safety” when the FDA continues to warn people not to use the product is certainly…a choice.

And now the Senate wants answers. The rest of the industry gets to live with the consequences.
