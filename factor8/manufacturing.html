The manufacturing record.

Company statements, sworn affidavits and government allegations each have a distinct evidentiary role.

Start with the manufacturer’s own account.

E18PDF pp. 6–7

Industrial processing described by the company

The publication reports a documentary shot in American Shaman’s Kansas City facility and describes conversion beginning with kratom leaf extract. The account is first-party evidence of the company’s description, not an independent inspection or a batch record. [E18 · PDF pp. 6–7]

E23¶¶ 10–17 · PDF pp. 4–5

Sworn statements about the business

The affidavit describes 7-OH products, sales, infrastructure, personnel and testing. These are useful commercial facts even though the affidavit does not provide a named production lot’s input/output record. [E23 · ¶¶ 10–17 · PDF pp. 4–5]

Separate allegations, testimony and unseen records.

The Missouri complaint against RRR alleges manufacturing and distribution relationships and discusses enforcement. Cite those propositions as allegations. The complaint is not itself a judicial finding that a particular process was used. [E21 · ¶¶ 41–50; PDF pp. 8–10]

Sanders says the original FDA response and attachments were filed with the company’s Answer. He separately says more than 9,000 pages were produced in response to a civil investigative demand. Neither collection is reproduced in this archive. They are precise record leads, not evidence whose unseen contents can be assumed. [E23 · ¶¶ 20 and 25; PDF pp. 6–7]

Do not merge corporate names without evidence.

Shaman Pharma LLC appears as the patent applicant. Sanders names Shaman Botanicals, CBD American Shaman and other companies in his affidavit. The petition does not assert that they are identical entities, share ownership or practice a patent without documentary support for that connection. [E17 · front page] [E23 · ¶ 2]
