Prong 2.
The immediate intermediary.

Separate preparative chemistry from human metabolism, and assess each controlled target on its own manufacturing route.

Finding requested · § 802(23)(B)

Mitragynine is an immediate chemical intermediary used or likely to be used in preparing 7-OH. The requested finding concerns the effectively controlled 7-OH target. MP pathways are retained as background, without a separate MP-based request. [L1]

“Immediate” limits the claim. “Likely” permits prevention.

The provision does not reach every chemical somewhere upstream of a controlled substance. The question is the molecule’s role at the immediate manufacturing stage. Yet the statute also includes an intermediary likely to be used. The petition need not wait for widespread illicit production of an already controlled drug to ask DEA to evaluate a documented, foreseeable route. [L1]

A process can involve transient reaction intermediates without making the named preparative input irrelevant. The legal assessment should distinguish those transient species from a separately prepared, commercially supplied compound between mitragynine and the target. This is the route-specific analysis requested of DEA, not an assertion that every route is legally equivalent.

For 7-OH, lead with preparation—not metabolism.

Mitragynine is the identified preparative input

The disclosure describes the preparation and characterization of the corresponding 7-hydroxy compound from mitragynine. It supports the direct preparative relationship rather than only a biological relationship. [E04 · Example 19 · PDF pp. 61–62]

E16Example 1 · ¶¶ 301–303

The same input/product relationship in another disclosure

The application includes ordinary 7-OH preparation from mitragynine. Its relevance is the immediate relationship described in the preparation, not unrelated therapeutic assertions. [E16 · Example 1 · ¶¶ 301–303]

The conversion-method grants expressly name the same input and product. Their related-family status is disclosed; three grants do not become three independent laboratory replications. [E08 · Claim 1] [E09 · Claim 1] [E10 · Claim 1]

North Dakota forensic testimony corroborates the chemical relationship.

On August 12, 2026, Charlene Rittenbach, Senior Forensic Scientist and Forensic Chemistry Technical Leader in the North Dakota Attorney General’s Crime Laboratory Division, submitted testimony to the Kratom Working Group. Page 1 states that “7-OH can be synthesized from Mitragynine through a chemical reaction”. [H02 · p.1]

This is attributed technical corroboration of the input/product relationship. The worked preparations already cited supply the route-specific evidence of immediacy; the hearing statement alone does not document every reaction stage, identify the principal commercial route or establish the concentration of a manufactured target.

The same submission discusses biological conversion and trace botanical 7-OH. Those observations are not substituted for industrial synthesis. Its product-label findings and explanation of threshold treatment are addressed under Prong C, with their limitations. [H02 · pp.2, 4–5]

MP pathways provide context; 7-OH is the sole petition target.

Different pseudoindoxyl speciesThe discussion of formula IV is not silently substituted for the MP identified as formula III. Exact identity and stereochemistry must match the target DEA controls. [E17 · ¶¶ 75–80; PDF pp. 12–13]

Biological conversion is supporting context.

The active-metabolite and human-plasma papers support a relationship among mitragynine, 7-OH and MP in biological systems. They are not factory records. The petition does not treat ingestion or human metabolism as proof of the industrial manufacture covered by its precursor theory. [E24] [E42]

Similarly, a claim that mitragynine is a “prodrug” does not itself decide the three statutory findings. The manufacturing relationship must be supported on its own terms.

Do not conceal the routes that complicate the case.

E53PDF pp. 1–6

An alternative MP total synthesis is in this archive

Angyal and colleagues report a scalable total synthesis of kratom pseudoindoxyl metabolites. The petition does not assert that every possible MP route must pass through mitragynine. Whether this alternative is commercially adopted or practically interchangeable is not established by this archive. [E53 · PDF pp. 1–6]

A different source of mitragynine is a different issue: microbial or nonbotanical production can still yield the very molecule proposed for control. A route that makes mitragynine without leaf does not necessarily bypass mitragynine as a precursor. [E33 · PDF pp. 1–3] [E31]

Proposed agency finding.

The preparative record supports a finding that mitragynine is an immediate chemical intermediary used or likely to be used in the manufacture of 7-hydroxymitragynine. The petition does not request a separate MP-based designation. The petition relies on documented chemical relationships, not on treating all upstream materials or all metabolic relationships as immediate precursors.

The next inquiry is not whether this chemistry exists. It is why control of this input is necessary to restrict the relevant manufacture. Continue to Prong 3.
