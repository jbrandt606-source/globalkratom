I. Request for agency action.

The undersigned petitions the Administrator of the Drug Enforcement Administration to designate mitragynine as an immediate precursor under 21 U.S.C. § 802(23) and to place it in Schedule I pursuant to 21 U.S.C. § 811(e) and 21 CFR § 1308.47, upon the findings described below. This request is submitted in the format prescribed by 21 CFR § 1308.43, with proposed regulatory text and a statement of grounds. [L1] [L2] [L3] [L4]

The requested designation concerns mitragynine’s role in manufacturing mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MP) and, upon effective federal control of the relevant substance, 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). The two target pathways are severable. The petitioner requests a written determination on each rather than an assumption that evidence concerning one necessarily resolves the other.

The petitioner seeks control of mitragynine itself and its salts, including material, compounds, mixtures and preparations containing the molecule. The requested breadth and its consequences for botanical material are expressly addressed in Section VII. The request does not ask DEA to assign controlled status to unrelated upstream precursors merely because they precede mitragynine.

II. Concise statement of grounds.

Published preparative disclosures identify mitragynine as an input to 7-OH. A company-authored account describes industrial processing of kratom extract into 7-OH, and a sworn affidavit establishes commercial production, infrastructure and testing. Analytical research provides additional context concerning commercial formulations. These sources provide a concrete basis to evaluate actual or likely use, rather than a speculative association among similarly named compounds. [E04 · Example 19; PDF pp. 61–62] [E16 · Example 1; ¶¶ 301–303] [E18 · PDF p. 7] [E23 · ¶¶ 10–17] [E48 · PDF pp. 5–9]

The requested necessity finding is prospective: controlling the identified input would apply controlled-substance safeguards before conversion into the target product. The petition does not claim that input control would eliminate every alternative synthesis, that all mitragynine is produced for conversion, or that every commercial label establishes a manufacturing method. It asks DEA to assess the expected restriction of the documented routes and the appropriate scope of control.

III. Statutory authority and history.

Factor 8, § 811(c)(8), identifies immediate-precursor status as a consideration in ordinary scheduling. The separate operative authority is § 811(e). It permits control without the ordinary findings specified in § 811(a) and § 812(b) and the procedures specified in § 811(a) and (b), but still requires satisfaction of § 802(23). [L2]

The authority originated in the 1970 Controlled Substances Act. DEA’s 1979 phenylacetone order explained the need to regulate a chemical input separately when it stood immediately before production of controlled drugs. ANPP in 2010 and norfentanyl in 2020 provide additional examples of this statutory mechanism. All three were Schedule II actions; they are cited for the mechanism and reasoning, not represented as Schedule I precedents. [L6] [L7] [L8]

The statutory terms “used or likely to be used” and “prevent, curtail, or limit” authorize a forward-looking determination. The petitioner does not contend that these words eliminate the principal-compound, immediacy or necessity findings. The contention is that widespread illicit production is not an additional prerequisite written into the statute. [L1]

IV. Controlled targets and timing.

As reflected in the October 14, 2026 notice, MP was placed in temporary Schedule I on August 26, 2026, and that order remains in force until expiration or replacement and withdrawal. The October 14 publication itself is a notice of intent addressing replacement control of specified 7-OH/MP preparations. It is not an effective replacement order. [L9]

Accordingly, the MP request proceeds only on an applicable effective target designation. The 7-OH request is contingent upon effective federal control of the relevant target substance. Before granting relief, DEA is requested to identify the operative target order, chemical identity, covered preparations and schedule, including any threshold or exception. No proposed threshold is treated as already operative merely because it appears in a notice.

If the target’s controlling order changes while this request is pending, the petitioner requests an opportunity to supplement the record and seeks relief consistent with § 811(e)’s schedule-matching rule. A Schedule I target permits Schedule I precursor placement; the petition does not assert that DEA must choose that schedule automatically. [L2]

V. Findings requested under § 802(23).

A. The principal compound used in manufacture.

The petitioner relies on the “principal compound used” branch of Prong A. Columbia Example 19 reports a preparation and characterization of ordinary 7-OH from mitragynine; the Kures disclosure provides another preparative passage; the conversion-method claims name the same input and product. Related patent-family publications are not counted as independent replications. [E04 · Example 19] [E16 · ¶¶ 301–303] [E08 · Claim 1]

American Shaman’s production account and Sanders’ affidavit supply commercial context. Avula’s analytical study supplies selected product-composition evidence. Together these sources support a principal-input inference. They do not purport to establish a quantified national production share or the content of unseen batch records. [E18 · PDF p. 7] [E23 · ¶¶ 10–17] [E48 · PDF pp. 5–9]

For MP, the petitioner requests a separate finding on the mitragynine-to-MP route disclosed in WO2025123039A1, paragraph 75, rather than assuming that a route involving a separately prepared 7-OH intermediary establishes every aspect of Prong A. [E17 · ¶¶ 71–75]

B. An immediate chemical intermediary used or likely to be used.

The preparative record supports a direct mitragynine-to-7-OH relationship. The legal theory rests on manufacture, not solely on mitragynine’s metabolic conversion after ingestion. Actual or likely use is supported by the combination of preparative disclosure and commercial context. [E04 · Example 19] [E18 · PDF p. 7] [L1]

For MP, DEA should distinguish the route through 7-OH from the separate paragraph 75 embodiment, verify that formula III matches the controlled target, and assess any legally significant intervening compound. The disclosure of a different formula IV pseudoindoxyl is not substituted for evidence about MP. [E17 · PDF pp. 11–13]

C. Control necessary to prevent, curtail or limit manufacture.

The petitioner submits that an unregulated supply of the identified input preserves a foreseeable means of producing the controlled targets through the supported routes. Scheduling mitragynine would add direct controlled-substance requirements at the input stage, including applicable registration and recordkeeping obligations and safeguards against diversion. The asserted effect is restriction of uncontrolled acquisition before conversion, not complete elimination of manufacture. [L11] [L12] [L8 · necessity finding, 85 FR 21321]

This inference is grounded in the described commercial processing of kratom extract, the established preparative relationship, and evidence of a market for the resulting products. It does not depend on proving that a particular volume of illicit post-scheduling production has already occurred. DEA is requested to evaluate the expected restriction alongside legitimate uses, alternative pathways and the breadth of the proposed rule. [E18 · PDF pp. 6–7] [E37 · PDF pp. 6–7] [L1]

VI. Material qualifications and contrary evidence.

Alternative MP synthesis. Angyal and colleagues report an alternative total synthesis of pseudoindoxyl metabolites. The petitioner does not claim that mitragynine is the only conceivable route to MP. The archive does not establish this alternative’s current commercial share or economic substitutability. DEA should assess whether restricting the supported mitragynine route would nevertheless prevent, curtail or limit manufacture. [E53 · PDF pp. 1–6]

Nonbotanical mitragynine sources. Biosynthetic or total-synthetic production of mitragynine is different from a route bypassing the molecule. Such sources should be assessed according to whether they produce the proposed precursor, not dismissed merely because they do not involve harvested leaf. [E33] [E31]

Botanical pharmacology. The record includes studies differentiating mitragynine from 7-OH and controlled studies of dried kratom leaf. Their findings remain limited to the tested preparations and conditions. The petitioner does not convert those papers into findings that every preparation has identical pharmacology, nor rely on that proposition to satisfy § 802(23). [E28] [E30]

Evidence provenance. A patent is not independent confirmation of commercial adoption. A COA is not a matched input/output record. A complaint contains allegations. UNODC’s manufacturing statement attributes its underlying proposition to Hill, so the two are not counted as separate factory investigations. Analytical identification also requires adequate discrimination among related compounds. [E73] [E21 · ¶¶ 41–50] [E22 · PDF p. 1] [E32]

Entity identity. Shaman Pharma LLC is the named patent applicant. The petition does not assume it is the same entity as Shaman Botanicals or another company in Sanders’ affidavit without supporting evidence. [E17 · front page] [E23 · ¶ 2]

VII. Requested scope, alternatives and severability.

The primary proposal covers mitragynine and its salts in any material, compound, mixture or preparation. The petitioner requests this scope because the manufacturing record includes mitragynine-containing extracts, not merely pure isolated material. Leaving the same molecule available through extract or botanical preparations could preserve the input pathway that precursor control is intended to restrict. This is the petitioner’s proposed necessity inference; it should be expressly evaluated by DEA. [E18 · PDF p. 7] [E17 · ¶¶ 9–16]

The requested scope would affect mitragynine-containing consumer preparations, including botanical products. The petitioner requests consideration of those consequences, legitimate research, analytical enforceability and any supported exception. No numerical cutoff is proposed without an adequate record, and no existing exception is silently removed. This is not precursor-of-a-precursor scheduling: the requested coverage follows the presence of the designated molecule, subject to the findings and limits of § 811(e). [L2]

If DEA finds a supported narrower scope or one target relationship established but not the other, the petitioner requests a partial grant or an opportunity to amend rather than denial of the entire request solely because another severable theory is unresolved. If DEA declines precursor relief, the petitioner requests a written explanation identifying the finding or scope not supported and consideration of the evidence under any separately pending ordinary scheduling proceeding. This record is not represented as a completed alternative eight-factor petition.

VIII. Proposed regulatory text.

The petitioner proposes to amend 21 CFR § 1308.11 by adding a new paragraph (i), entitled “Immediate precursors,” as follows. Existing paragraphs (a)–(h) would remain unchanged. Paragraph numbering should be reconciled with any intervening amendment before filing or promulgation. The DEA administrative code is to be assigned by the agency. [L13]

§ 1308.11 — Schedule I.

(i) Immediate precursors. Unless specifically excepted or unless listed in another schedule, any material, compound, mixture, or preparation which contains any quantity of the following substance, or any of its salts whenever the existence of such salts is possible within the specific chemical designation:

(1) Mitragynine.

This is requested regulatory text, not an existing listing. The accompanying order should expressly designate mitragynine as an immediate precursor of each target for which DEA makes affirmative findings under § 802(23), identify the operative controlled target and schedule, and explain the scope adopted. The amendment should take effect only after those findings and within the limits of § 811(e). It does not create automatic future control based on a notice of intent. [L1] [L2]

The petitioner requests the ordinary effective-date treatment in § 1308.47 unless DEA makes the findings necessary for an earlier date. For the filing copy, attach the current text of § 1308.11 alongside this proposed amendment, as required by the petition-content rule for an amendment to an existing regulation. [L3] [L4] [L13]

IX. Additional records and requested determination.

The petitioner requests that DEA consider relevant manufacturing and supply-chain records already held by federal or state agencies, to the extent lawfully available, and obtain any additional information necessary for a reasoned decision. Specifically relevant are records identifying the commercial input, specifications, lots, distribution and any practical alternative production route.

Sanders’ affidavit states that the original August 19, 2025 FDA warning-letter response and attachments accompanied American Shaman’s Answer, and separately describes a production of over 9,000 pages to Missouri. Those underlying materials are not included here and are not represented as supporting unseen facts. [E23 · ¶¶ 20 and 25; PDF pp. 6–7]

The petitioner respectfully requests acceptance for filing, an affirmative determination on each supported statutory element, and issuance of an order within DEA’s authority if those findings are made. If additional information is required, the petitioner requests identification of the specific issue and an opportunity to supplement. A written decision should distinguish chemical-role findings, necessity, scope and the status of the controlled target.
