FACTOR EIGHT / The three findings / A

Prong 1.
The principal compound.

The evidence must identify mitragynine’s role in making the controlled target—not merely its presence in kratom.

Finding requested · § 802(23)(A)

Mitragynine is the principal compound used in the documented preparations of 7-OH. This petition relies solely on the effectively controlled 7-OH target; MP material is retained as background. The case relies on the principal compound used branch, not an unsupported claim that most mitragynine worldwide is produced for this purpose. [L1]

The question is what chemical is being made into the target.

Prong A is not satisfied merely because mitragynine is abundant in the plant. The relevant evidence identifies it as the chemical input that becomes the target substance. Nor does the statutory text prescribe a purity cutoff or a particular kind of batch record as the exclusive form of proof. Whether the total record supports the required finding remains an agency determination. [L1]

The preparative record identifies mitragynine.

E04Example 19 · PDF pp. 61–62

A worked preparation, not just a general claim

The Columbia disclosure reports preparation and characterization of ordinary 7-OH from mitragynine. This is direct evidence of the input-to-product relationship in preparative chemistry. The related continuation repeats the family’s disclosure and should not be counted as another independent experiment. [E04 · Example 19 · PDF pp. 61–62]

E16Example 1 · ¶¶ 301–303

A second preparative disclosure

The Kures application includes preparation of ordinary 7-OH from mitragynine within a larger salts-and-polymorphs disclosure. The preparative passage is relevant even though the overall patent subject is different. [E16 · Example 1 · ¶¶ 301–303]

E08Claim 1 · PDF pp. 2–4

Claims expressly directed to conversion

The method claim expressly names mitragynine as the input and 7-OH as the resulting product. It supports the disclosed chemical role. Grant of the patent is not independent proof of successful manufacture at every claimed scale. [E08 · Claim 1 · PDF pp. 2–4]

Commercial evidence connects the chemistry to a real business.

E18PDF pp. 6–7

A company describes its production process

American Shaman’s own publication describes industrial 7-OH production beginning with kratom leaf extract, followed by conversion, analysis and purification at its Kansas City facility. That is a first-party description of manufacture, not merely a retailer’s finished-product label. [E18 · PDF pp. 6–7]

E23¶¶ 10–17 · PDF pp. 4–5

The owner describes a manufacturing enterprise

Sanders describes separately formulated kratom-derived products, 7-OH sales beginning in late 2023, manufacturing infrastructure, chemists and release testing. This supports actual commercial context. The cited paragraphs do not identify a particular production batch’s mitragynine input. [E23 · ¶¶ 10–17 · PDF pp. 4–5]

Combined inference: the worked chemistry identifies the molecule; the company account describes commercial processing of its botanical source; the affidavit establishes the enterprise. Together they support a principal-input finding more strongly than any item alone. They do not establish a national market share or a matched input/output chain of custody.

Product analysis supplies corroboration, not a factory ledger.

E48PDF pp. 5–9

Analytical patterns in commercial formulations

The paper examines commercial samples and discusses patterns consistent with semisynthetic processing and formulations derived from mitragynine or enriched extracts. It adds chemical analysis to the record. A selected sample set cannot establish every product’s production history or identify every supplier. [E48 · PDF pp. 5–9]

E37PDF pp. 6–7

The manufacturing inference in the published market study

Hill and colleagues reason that the quantities and difficulty of isolating natural 7-OH strongly suggest laboratory manufacture from purified mitragynine. Their study catalogued marketed products; it did not chemically verify those products. [E37 · PDF pp. 6–7]

UNODC later repeats the manufacturing proposition with attribution to Hill. That is authoritative recognition of the concern, but not a separate factory investigation. [E22 · PDF p. 1]

What the hearing evidence contributes to Prong A.

Rittenbach’s filed North Dakota testimony identifies chemical synthesis of 7-OH from mitragynine. That corroborates the identity of a possible input; it does not measure how much commercial production uses that input. Her chemistry belongs principally under Prong B. [H02 · p.1]

Stuart’s Kansas statement connects alkaloid scheduling with manufacturing deterrence and source material. Its principal use is Prong C. It is not a manufacturer’s input ledger or a national production survey. [H01 · p.2]

Prong A therefore continues to rest on the preparative and commercial record above. Additional investigative material would strengthen this prong if it identifies actual feedstock purchases, conversion operations and the principal compound used. General support for a ban does not establish those facts.

MP disclosure remains background to the 7-OH petition.

WO2025123039A1 distinguishes a route involving 7-OH from another embodiment proceeding from mitragynine, formula I, to MP, formula III. Paragraph 75 supplies relevant disclosure for an MP-related finding. It is not proof that a particular retail batch followed that embodiment. [E17 · ¶¶ 71–75; PDF pp. 11–12]

The named patent applicant is Shaman Pharma LLC. Sanders identifies other legal entities in his affidavit. The petition does not assume common ownership, patent use or a corporate relationship solely from a shared word in the names. [E23 · ¶ 2; PDF p. 2]

Proposed agency finding.

The record supports a finding that mitragynine is the principal compound used in the documented manufacture of 7-hydroxymitragynine. This conclusion rests on preparative examples, disclosures expressly identifying the input and product, and corroborating commercial and analytical evidence. The requested 7-OH designation is contingent on effective control of the target substance. No separate MP-based designation is requested in the accompanying petition.

An authenticated manufacturer input statement would further strengthen this inference. It is not presented as a statutory requirement that must be satisfied before DEA can consider the evidence. Next: establish chemical immediacy.
