Part VI: The Necessity Finding

The statutory case for preventive control, and the evidence DEA would need to evaluate.

Factor Eight / Part VI: The Necessity Finding

Prevent, curtail, or limit

The statute is preventive. It does not require a petitioner to wait for widespread illicit production. But the record must explain how controlling mitragynine would restrict manufacture of a specified controlled substance.

A route-specific argument

Demonstrated chemical conversion, access to precursor materials and commercial production can support a prospective necessity finding. Alternative synthesis routes must be acknowledged.

The question of scope

The case for controlling isolated mitragynine and the consequences for botanical preparations must be addressed explicitly. The existence of a chemical precursor relationship does not resolve every question about regulatory scope.

Source distinction: patents, company statements, scientific findings and litigation allegations are not interchangeable. The proposed DEA findings have not been made by the agency.
