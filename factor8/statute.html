The law reaches an earlier point in manufacture.

A controlled substance is a drug or other substance placed in a federal schedule. An immediate precursor is a chemical input that meets a particular three-part legal definition. Congress made it possible to control that input because controlling only the finished substance may leave a means of producing it outside the controlled-substance system. [L1] [L2]

The distinction is straightforward: the ordinary scheduling question asks about the substance itself; the immediate-precursor question asks about its role in manufacturing a substance already controlled. An input need not have the same independent abuse profile as the finished drug for the separate authority to apply. [L2] [L8 · 85 FR 21321–21322]

Factor 8 is not an eight-part precursor test.

Section 811(c) lists eight considerations used in ordinary scheduling: abuse potential; pharmacological effects; current scientific knowledge; history and pattern of abuse; scope, duration and significance of abuse; public-health risk; dependence liability; and immediate-precursor status. The eighth asks whether the substance is an immediate precursor of a substance already controlled. [L2]

The three prongs on this site are paragraphs (A), (B) and (C) of § 802(23). Section 811(e) supplies the separate authority to schedule a qualifying immediate precursor. These provisions work together, but they are not interchangeable.

The three findings, word for word.

21 U.S.C. § 802(23). The term “immediate precursor” means a substance—

(A) which the Attorney General has found to be and by regulation designated as being the principal compound used, or produced primarily for use, in the manufacture of a controlled substance;

(B) which is an immediate chemical intermediary used or likely to be used in the manufacture of such controlled substance; and

(C) the control of which is necessary to prevent, curtail, or limit the manufacture of such controlled substance. [L1]

ProngPlain-English questionEvidence to examine
A · Principal compoundIs this the principal chemical used in making the controlled target, or is it produced primarily for that use?Preparative records, relevant patents, manufacturing statements and input records.
B · Immediate intermediaryIs the chemical directly involved at the immediate manufacturing stage, actually or foreseeably?Route-specific chemistry, exact compound identities and any separately prepared intervening compounds.
C · Necessary controlWhy would controlling this input prevent, restrict or reduce manufacture of that target?Supply access, regulatory controls, actual or likely production, alternatives and the scope of the requested order.

“Likely to be used” means preventive action is permitted.

Prong B expressly includes likely use; Prong C expressly includes prevention. Neither provision states a minimum volume of illicit production, a required number of deaths, or a requirement that the relevant manufacturing already be widespread. The petition relies on those words, not on an invented mass-production threshold. [L1]

That does not make every possible starting material an immediate precursor. Prong A still requires the principal-compound or primary-production finding. Prong B still requires chemical immediacy. Prong C still requires a supported explanation of necessity. A merely conceivable conversion, far removed from the controlled product, is not enough by itself.

What § 811(e) authorizes—and what it does not.

The Attorney General may place a qualifying immediate precursor in the same schedule as the target substance, or one with a higher numerical designation. Thus a Schedule I target permits a Schedule I precursor; a Schedule II target does not by itself authorize Schedule I precursor control under this provision. [L2]

The provision dispenses with the ordinary findings under § 811(a) and § 812(b), and the procedures specified in § 811(a) and (b). It does not dispense with the three-part precursor definition. DEA’s implementing regulation is § 1308.47. It ordinarily provides a 30-day period between publication of a precursor order and effectiveness, with an earlier date permitted upon the specified public-health or safety finding. [L3]

Congress also barred automatic upward chaining: a different substance cannot be scheduled solely because it is a precursor of a substance newly designated an immediate precursor. This petition therefore evaluates mitragynine’s relationship to the controlled target itself; it does not ask DEA to schedule every upstream chemical. [L2]

What the petition asks to control.

The requested subject is the mitragynine molecule, not a brand, a marketing claim or a product nickname. The proposed text includes mitragynine and its salts in materials, mixtures and preparations, including botanical material containing the molecule. That is a requested scope of federal control, not a statement of existing law. The necessity of that scope is argued on the Prong 3 page.

An ingredient’s origin does not by itself answer the statutory questions. Conversely, proof that an isolated input is used in manufacture does not remove the need to explain the consequences of controlling all mitragynine-containing preparations. The proposed petition makes that issue express.

Acceptance for filing is not approval.

Section 1308.43 calls for a proposed rule, a reasonably concise statement of grounds including known relevant scientific or medical evidence, a petitioner’s name and address, and a signature. It distinguishes acceptance for filing from a later decision about whether proceedings are justified. [L4]

The petition page supplies proposed text and grounds. Personal contact and signature fields must be completed in the filing copy, and the operative target scheduling order must be checked at filing. Publishing this website does not itself file anything with DEA.
