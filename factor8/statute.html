Part I: The Law

Three findings, one preventive authority, and the limits of the immediate-precursor pathway.

Factor Eight / Part I: The Law

Prong One: Principal compound

The compound must be a principal compound commonly used, or produced primarily for use, in the manufacture of a controlled substance. A documented chemical pathway is relevant; it should not be confused with proof of national market share.

Prong Two: Immediate intermediary

The substance must be an immediate chemical intermediary used or likely to be used in manufacture. The prospective language is important: the statute does not expressly require a completed commercial batch record.

Prong Three: Necessary control

Control must be necessary to prevent, curtail or limit manufacture. That requires an explanation of the effect of controlling mitragynine, not merely evidence that the finished compounds are hazardous.

Separate authority

Section 811(e) permits immediate-precursor control without ordinary scheduling findings. The requested scope and the current schedule of each target compound must be established.

Source distinction: patents, company statements, scientific findings and litigation allegations are not interchangeable. The proposed DEA findings have not been made by the agency.
