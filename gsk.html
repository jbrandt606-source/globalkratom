Why Did a Major Drug Company Abandon Mitragynine Research? Historical Records Point to Toxicity

Smith Kline & French investigated kratom alkaloids in the 1960s. Historical records point to unacceptable acute effects—and leave a crucial toxicology report unaccounted for.

HISTORICAL INVESTIGATION · NOVEMBER 22, 2026

What happened to the mitragynine drug program?

Smith Kline & French Laboratories (SKF), a predecessor in the corporate history of today's GSK, investigated alkaloids from Mitragyna speciosa during the 1960s. The program produced patents and published pharmacology. Yet mitragynine did not emerge from the program as a marketed analgesic.

The most consequential evidence is not a theory about profitability. An Australian regulatory record from 2004 reports that a company confirmed an earlier account that preclinical investigation was discontinued because of unacceptable acute effects. A scientist interviewed in 2017 separately identified toxicity observed in beagle dogs. The underlying original company toxicology report has not been located in the sources reviewed here.

What the evidence supports: A historically documented research program, a regulatory record attributing confirmation of adverse effects to a company, and a later specific recollection of dog toxicity. What it does not establish: the precise adverse reaction, affected dose, pathology, human clinical findings, or the complete internal decision-making record.

PRIMARY DOCUMENTS · AUSTRALIAN GOVERNMENT · 2003–2004

What the government records actually say

The most important finding is recorded on printed page 73 of the June 2004 National Drugs and Poisons Schedule Committee (NDPSC) record. The committee describes a company's confirmation of an earlier account of unacceptable acute effects during preclinical mitragynine work. The published record redacts the company name.

EXHIBIT A · JUNE 2004 · PRINTED PAGE 73 · PDF PAGE 74

Company confirmation recorded

“The company confirmed the report that the pre-clinical trial apparently revealed unacceptable acute effects”

The committee continues by recording that the investigation was discontinued. This is a government summary of a company's reported confirmation, not the underlying toxicology report or an unredacted identification of SKF.

Regulators sought the missing documentation

The committee suggested that its secretariat contact the redacted company for documentation associated with the mitragynine investigation. The 2003 summary describes early-1970s human work, whereas the 2004 account calls the work 1960s preclinical research. That discrepancy is unresolved.

THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Why did SKF abandon mitragynine?

01

Australian regulators recorded company confirmation

The June 2004 National Drugs and Poisons Schedule Committee record reports confirmation by a company of the earlier account that its preclinical investigation was stopped after unacceptable acute effects. The published record redacts the company name. Its connection to SKF is supported by the surrounding historical research record, but the redaction should not be represented as direct identification.

02

A scientist recalled toxicity in beagle dogs

In a 2017 ASBMB article, David Phillipson described SKF's interest in mitragynine as an analgesic and recalled toxicity in beagle dogs as the obstacle that ended development. This is a retrospective account, not a recovered original animal-study report.

03

An earlier paper described unacceptable side effects

A 1988 historical account traces the assertion about unacceptable side effects to a 1986 personal communication from R. Raffauf. It is an important provenance trail, but does not independently identify specific adverse events or provide clinical case records.

04

The original toxicology remains the missing piece

The 2003 Australian committee record points toward obtaining original company documentation. The available public sources cited here do not establish whether regulators received the raw study, a company letter, or only a confirmation. That distinction is central to any stronger conclusion.

Historical caution: “Abandoned research” means the company's development effort did not proceed to a marketed mitragynine medicine. It does not mean all mitragynine research ceased, nor does it prove that mitragynine can never be developed into an approved drug.

The missing toxicology report: what did SKF actually find?

More than sixty years after a major pharmaceutical company investigated mitragynine, the original toxicology findings reportedly associated with the end of development remain unavailable in the sources reviewed. What did the scientists observe, and where are those reports today?

  • Animal toxicology: Beagle-dog study protocols, dose groups, clinical signs, laboratory data, pathology and final reports.
  • Development decisions: Internal memoranda recommending discontinuation, safety committee minutes, and correspondence between project scientists.
  • Australian regulator correspondence: Documents received or requested in connection with NDPSC Meetings 37 and 41, including the identity of the redacted company.
  • Historical correspondence: The basis of R. Raffauf's 1986 communication and any surviving communications with the SKF researchers.
The central unanswered questions are which adverse effects occurred, at what doses and exposures, in which species or people, and what the company concluded. The Australian records make this a concrete archival investigation—not proof of a particular human toxicity or a complete account of the company’s decision.
