OPMS Kratom: The Defense Asks to Dismiss the Case

What the defendants argue, what the exhibits actually show, and why the difference matters for lawmakers.

In the wrongful-death lawsuit brought by Kathleen Moller following her daughter Harmony Moller’s death, the OPMS defendants filed a motion asking the federal court to rule in their favor without a trial.

The motion was filed March 2, 2026, in the Eastern District of Louisiana, Case No. 2:24-cv-00228-WBV-DPC. This is the defendants’ account of the evidence. It is not a court ruling and it does not establish that kratom is safe.

At a Glance

  • What is filed: A motion for summary judgment, legal memorandum, defense statement of proposed facts, and 75 supporting attachments.
  • What defendants seek: Dismissal of the plaintiff’s claims without a trial.
  • What is disputed: Whether OPMS kratom caused or contributed to Harmony Moller’s death, whether product warnings were adequate, and which companies bear legal responsibility.
  • Why it matters: The record contains product labels, emergency records, testimony, scientific research, regulatory material and competing expert opinions.
  • Critical caution: The unsigned proposed order in Exhibit 267-76 is a proposed result written for the judge, not a signed judgment.

What lawmakers should examine first

1. The product sold to consumers. The OPMS packaging exhibits include an ingredient representation of kratom leaf and listed alkaloid concentrations. A label declaration is not a laboratory confirmation or a safety assessment.

2. The death investigation. The emergency-response and medical evidence establish what was documented, but determining medical causation requires expert interpretation and consideration of other possible contributors.

3. The scientific dispute. The record contains research on kratom toxicology, cardiovascular findings and adverse-event reporting. The parties disagree about what these studies establish for this individual case.

4. The warnings question. Product-label photographs and Dr. Sarah Kowitt’s expert report provide a basis for examining whether consumers were given meaningful information about serious potential harms.

5. The limits of a short-term study. Preliminary single-dose research is not proof that repeated use, vulnerable consumers, or combinations with other drugs are safe.

Essential distinctionDefendants can argue that the plaintiff has not proved causation without proving that kratom is harmless. Those are different legal and scientific questions.

How to read the defense’s arguments

The defendants contend that the available evidence does not satisfy the legal requirements for the plaintiff’s claims. Their motion asks the judge to assess whether there is a genuine dispute requiring trial.

Some exhibits are expert opinions; some are selected deposition excerpts; others are scientific papers, regulatory materials, or news articles. Their presence in the court record does not mean the judge adopted every statement in them.

For lawmakers, the most useful approach is to read the primary documents and separate documented facts from allegations, competing interpretations and policy claims.
