Framework notes
• National Leaders: states with full retail bans, Schedule I classification for mitragynine, or functional suppression (no commercial availability).
• Getting There: states actively moving toward meaningful suppression — unanimous pharmacy board scheduling votes, pending legislation, local‑ban authority, or regulatory pathways that restrict retail access.
• Weak Systems: states with partial regulation (e.g., age restrictions, basic labeling) but no effective suppression; broad retail normalization persists.
• National Failures: states with either high poison‑center / autopsy burden AND permissive retail environments, or KCPA frameworks that failed to reduce harm (Addiction, 2026).
• Nevada’s placement in “Getting There” reflects the unanimous Board of Pharmacy scheduling vote, a stronger signal than KCPA alone. Minnesota moved to “National Failures” due to elevated burden and permissive retail normalization despite legislative activity.
