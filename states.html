State Policy Landscape

Kratom regulation, retail suppression, and public health outcomes across the United States – combining policy strength with CDC surveillance data and peer-reviewed findings.

Key Finding – Addiction (2026)
“States with consumer protection acts designed to promote safety experienced similar rates of severe outcomes as states with no regulation at all.” Retail bans and local suppression measures were significantly more effective in reducing healthcare utilization and poison center calls.

CDC SUDORS Overlay

States with full bans or strong local suppression have significantly lower mitragynine‑detected deaths per capita. The Addiction (2026) study confirms: “States with at least local restrictions had somewhat lower healthcare utilization compared with unrestricted states.”

Methodological Notes & Caveats

  • Texas appears in National Failures due to its extremely permissive retail environment (thousands of smoke shops, no state restrictions, major distribution hub) and lack of comprehensive SUDORS reporting; public health burden is likely underestimated.
  • Rhode Island reversed a de facto ban and adopted KCPA; despite currently lower SUDORS counts, the policy reversal represents a failure to maintain effective suppression, and retail normalization has increased.
  • Nevada is classified as “Getting There” because the Board of Pharmacy’s unanimous scheduling vote signals imminent suppression, even before final DEA action.
  • KCPA states (Georgia, Florida, etc.) often have higher autopsy and poison‑center burdens, consistent with the Addiction paper finding that KCPA did not reduce severe outcomes compared to no regulation.
