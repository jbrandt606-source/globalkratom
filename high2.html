Does Pure Leaf Kratom Get You High?

Part II – The FDA-Sponsored Human Trial

What the FDA-Funded Clinical Trial Found

One of the kratom industry's favorite talking points is that pure leaf kratom doesn't get you high.

The FDA funded a controlled clinical trial to answer that question.

Researchers administered botanical kratom leaf—not an extract, not a "spiked" product—to human volunteers. The kratom contained only trace amounts of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and was representative of ordinary leaf products.

The results were clear.

Participants receiving 12 grams of pure leaf kratom experienced statistically significant increases in:

  • Drug liking
  • Feeling "high"
  • Good drug effects
  • Overall drug liking
  • Desire to take the drug again

Drug liking increased from 50.2 with placebo to 68.7 with kratom. Feeling "high" increased from 0.4 with placebo to 46.3, with some participants rating their high as 94 out of 100.

The investigators concluded that the highest dose of botanical kratom produced effects commonly associated with drugs of abuse, including significant increases in drug liking and feeling high.

Conversion to 7-OH in the Body

The study also answered another important question.

Although the botanical leaf contained only trace amounts of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), every dose produced measurable 7-OH in participants' blood. Blood concentrations increased as the kratom dose increased, demonstrating that the human body converts mitragynine from ordinary leaf into the much more potent opioid metabolite 7-OH.

The takeaway is straightforward.

Pure botanical kratom is psychoactive. Under controlled clinical conditions, FDA-funded researchers found that ordinary leaf kratom produced opioid-like effects, measurable formation of 7-OH, significant drug liking, and a measurable high.

Do People Really Use 12 Grams of Kratom?

A common criticism of the FDA-sponsored human trial is that researchers evaluated a 12-gram dose of botanical kratom. But is that an unrealistic amount?

Screenshot from WebMD's Kratom Dosage Guide.

Not according to WebMD. Its consumer guide states that doses between about 5 and 15 grams of raw kratom may produce stronger effects. That places the 12-gram dose tested in the FDA-sponsored study squarely within the range that a major consumer health resource describes. The FDA researchers were not testing an impossible or hypothetical dose—they were evaluating an amount that public health resources already acknowledge people use.

Read the WebMD article here: https://www.webmd.com/diet/health-benefits-kratom

Don't Take Our Word for It

The data are public. The trial was conducted under rigorous clinical standards. And the conclusions are consistent with what thousands of kratom users have described in online forums: pure leaf kratom produces a high.

Below is a summary of key findings from the FDA-sponsored trial (full PDF linked above):

  • Drug liking: Placebo 50.2 → Kratom 68.7 (p < 0.05)
  • Feeling "high": Placebo 0.4 → Kratom 46.3 (range up to 94/100)
  • Good drug effects: significant increase at 12 g dose
  • Overall drug liking: dose-dependent increase
  • Desire to take again: significantly higher than placebo
  • 7-OH in blood: detectable after every dose, increasing with dose

These are not anecdotes. They are controlled clinical data.

What This Means for Policy

The FDA-sponsored trial provides clear evidence that pure botanical kratom is a psychoactive substance with abuse liability. The fact that ordinary leaf—not enhanced extracts—produces significant drug liking and a measurable high undermines claims that kratom is merely a coffee-like supplement.

Policymakers, healthcare providers, and the public deserve access to this evidence. The highlighted study (available above) makes the key findings easy to identify and share.
