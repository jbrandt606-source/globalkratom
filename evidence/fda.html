BD Botanicals Ltd. — Kratura (2019) | NDIN 1126

Natural dried kratom leaf powder. Filed August 15, 2019. The manufacturer proposed a maximum of 600 mg (0.6 grams) of leaf powder per day and excluded children, pregnant women and breastfeeding women. This was the company's proposed limit, not an FDA-established safe dose.

The company relied primarily on a 90-day rat study and acknowledged that traditional use alone was insufficient to establish the safety of its particular product. It also acknowledged that mitragynine and 7-OH act on opioid receptors. Much of the product-specific testing and composition information is redacted. The submission's literature review describes animal studies reporting liver damage, reduced platelet counts and physical dependence.

Document references: PDF pages 2–3, 11–13, 27–34 and 36–39. Manufacturer notification; FDA response letter is not included.

American Botanicals Corporation — Kratom Extract (2021)

Extract made from dried kratom leaves. Dated August 20, 2021. The company disputed FDA's position that a notification was required and acknowledged that FDA had raised concerns about its earlier December 21, 2017 submission. It argued that its extract was comparable to traditional kratom preparations and relied on historical use, animal testing, surveys and a PinneyAssociates assessment.

The dossier acknowledges that the body can convert mitragynine into 7-OH, citing animal and laboratory research, and identifies potential interactions with medicines including opioids and benzodiazepines. It also describes bacterial contamination, heavy metals, inconsistent potency and unclear labeling in commercial products. The company acknowledged that available published animal studies could not reliably establish a dose of mitragynine without adverse effects in rodents or humans.

Proposed serving amounts and much of the product-specific safety evidence are redacted. The company asserted that its product could lawfully be marketed regardless of FDA's response; this is the company's position, not an FDA finding.

Document references: PDF pages 4–6, 43–44, 53–59 and 67–71. Manufacturer notification; FDA response letter is not included.

FDA safety warnings & laboratory findings

FDA and Kratom – Official Overview

FDA states that kratom is not lawfully marketed as a dietary supplement or food additive and has no approved medical uses. Reported risks include liver damage, seizures, addiction and newborn withdrawal.

Read Statement →

Heavy Metals Laboratory Analysis

FDA tested 30 products and found lead and nickel at levels raising concerns about repeated exposure. Long-term exposure to these metals can damage the nervous system and kidneys and may increase cancer risk.

View Data →

Salmonella Outbreak Investigation

The outbreak investigation documented 199 illnesses across 41 states and 54 hospitalizations. Salmonella was found in 55% of FDA-tested samples.

Read Report →

2018 FDA Commissioner Statement

FDA Commissioner Scott Gottlieb warned about opioid activity and the potential for addiction and abuse.

Read Statement →

FDA PHASE Model Analysis (2018)

FDA used computer modeling to predict that 22 of 25 kratom compounds bind to the main opioid receptor. The agency also discussed 44 reported deaths. The modeling findings are predictions, not direct clinical measurements.
