Mortality & Severe Outcomes

Forensic toxicology reports, mortality surveillance, overdose investigations, and severe clinical outcomes associated with kratom and mitragynine exposure.

Key findings

Medical examiner investigations document deaths attributed to mitragynine, including cases without toxic levels of other drugs. Emergency reports also describe stopped breathing, cardiac arrest, organ failure and intensive care.

Iuteri et al. · Florida case-control study (May 2026)Researchers compared 38 deaths attributed to mitragynine with 111 deaths where it was detected but not considered causal. No other substance was at a toxic level in the 38 cases. Fluid in the lungs occurred in 55.6% versus 31.5%; enlarged hearts in 47.4% versus 11.8%.Read source →
Kliewer et al. · Oklahoma medical examiner study (2026)Mitragynine was listed in the cause of death in 22 of 28 investigations; 21 of those 22 also listed another drug.Read source →
CDC · Poison center surveillance (March 2026)Of 233 reported deaths during 2015–2025, 49 involved kratom alone as the reported exposure and 184 involved multiple substances. Reports are not independently adjudicated death certificates.Read source →
Leonard et al. · National poison center study (2026)Among 13,194 exposures from January 2016 through July 2025, 219 deaths were reported (43 single-substance and 176 multiple-substance). These data overlap with the CDC series and should not be added to it.Read source →
Eccarius et al. · Forensic laboratory testing (September 2026)Researchers developed a test distinguishing four kratom-related compounds and reported results from more than 200 actual cases. They found that 7-OH can break down in stored blood while pseudoindoxyl increases, complicating interpretation of death investigations.Read source →
Gerona et al. · Fatal concentrated 7-OH exposure (October 2026)A man died after reportedly using a concentrated 7-OH product and drinking alcohol. Testing identified 7-OH and pseudoindoxyl; autopsy found severe fluid accumulation in both lungs.Read source →
Pulmonary toxicity systematic review (2026)Review of 24 reports (14 clinical and 10 forensic) identified lung bleeding, severe lung injury, slowed breathing and fluid in lungs. Attribution was probable in nine reports, possible in 13 and unlikely in two.Read source →
Dunz et al. · Cardiac arrest after kratom tea (September 2026)A young man experienced cardiac arrest associated with isolated kratom intoxication, supported by blood testing. This is evidence of a potentially fatal emergency, not a confirmed death.Read source →
