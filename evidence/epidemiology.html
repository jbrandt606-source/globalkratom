2. How many people use kratom?

How many people use kratom?

National Survey on Drug Use and Health (NSDUH)

Estimated 1.7 million Americans aged ≥12 used kratom in 2021. Lifetime prevalence ~1.3%, past‑year 0.8% (≈2.1 million adults). Users were more often male and younger, and more often reported using multiple substances.

Read source →

Schimmel et al. (2021) – Cross‑sectional prevalence

First national U.S. prevalence estimate: 0.8% past‑year, 1.4% lifetime. Users reported more substance-use problems than non-users.

3. Exposure among children and older adults

Exposure among children and older adults

Older Adults (≥60 years)

From 2014–2019, 4.6% of kratom exposures (162 cases) were adults ≥60. Among ≥70 years, 21.9% were adverse reactions (drug interactions) – more than double the rate in younger adults. Effects involving the brain, nerves, heart and circulation were common; 23 deaths were reported.

Read source →

Pediatric Exposures (2019–2024)

Total pediatric cases: 707 (2019–2024). Children under 5 are the largest age group; 2024 saw a record 107 exposures in <5 yrs. Pediatric exposures increased from 102 (2019) to 150 (2024), driven almost entirely by the youngest children.

5. Exposure involving multiple substances

Exposure involving multiple substances

CDC Overdose Deaths (2016–2017)

152 deaths with kratom detected; 91 listed as cause. Fentanyl/fentanyl analogs present in 65%; heroin 33%; benzodiazepines 22%. Other drugs were frequently present, but kratom was listed as a cause in most of these cases.

Read source →

Post et al. (2019) – NPDS 2011–2017

Multiple‑substance exposures associated with 2.8× higher odds of hospital admission and 2.25× higher odds of serious medical outcome compared to single‑substance exposures.

Read source →

KCPA state regulation study (2026)

States with kratom bans had significantly lower exposure rates and severe outcomes than unrestricted or KCPA states, The study reports an association between state policy and reported harm.

Read source →
6. Medical examiner and toxicology studies

Medical examiner and toxicology studies

Nevada Kratom Study (2021)

35 kratom‑positive deaths; in one case mitragynine was the sole intoxicant (950 ng/mL). The wide range of blood concentrations in fatal cases does not establish a safe dose.

Read source →

Papsun et al. (2023) – Forensic Implications

Analysis of 6,860 blood specimens; mitragynine up to 11,000 ng/mL. Detection alongside other drugs with fentanyl (62%) and methamphetamine (19%). High blood concentrations (above 1,000 ng/mL) were associated with death.
