A Consignee Is Not Always the Retail Brand

The company named as consignee may be the buyer, importer, freight intermediary, warehouse, logistics provider, or another entity receiving or controlling the shipment.

The public record does not automatically establish beneficial ownership, importer-of-record status, downstream manufacturing, or the retail brand that ultimately used the material. It does identify the U.S. company name placed into the transport record and allows recurring supplier relationships to be measured.

Important: Consignee address and destination port are shipping-paperwork fields. They are not a map of final retail sales, consumer exposure, or the domestic route after the cargo left the port.

PUBLIC COMPANY RECORDS

What Publicly Available Information Adds

The workbook shows who appeared as consignee. Corporate filings, regulatory notices, and broader trade databases add context—but they do not automatically prove who owned the cargo or which retail brand ultimately used it.

Source rule: State corporate records are treated as entity-status evidence. Trade databases are treated as shipment evidence. A shared address, manager, or registered agent shows a documented corporate connection, but not necessarily common beneficial ownership, operational control, or a shared retail brand.
PROFILE 01

South Asia Trade

1,910.955 t45 curated records

What the workbook shows

  • Largest normalized consignee by documented weight.
  • Six suppliers, led by CV Cahaya and ZYAM Trading 168.
  • Forty records used 1211.90.
  • Four records used 3101.00 exclusively.
  • One additional record contained both 1211.90 and 3101.00.
  • Most records listed a Wyoming consignee address while most cargo arrived through Los Angeles.

What the public footprint shows

A readily verifiable consumer-facing website or substantial company-authored product catalog was not located during this review. The available public record is therefore dominated by shipment data rather than public product marketing.

The Wyoming address appearing in the shipping data should not be assumed to be the warehouse or final destination. A consignee address may be a corporate, administrative, registered-agent, or mailing address.

Why it matters: South Asia Trade is the only importer in the curated workbook associated with all four exclusively fertilizer-coded records and the one mixed-coded record. The public manifests do not explain why those headings were selected or whether CBP accepted, questioned, or later changed them.

What Florida corporate records show

Florida’s corporate database identifies International Packing Solutions, Inc. as a Florida profit corporation formed in 2005. The record lists a Doral address and Ricardo Herrero as an officer.

The state record shows an administrative dissolution for failure to file an annual report on September 26, 2025. That corporate status does not establish who owned or controlled cargo appearing under the name, and it does not by itself resolve why curated shipment records continue through February 2026.

What a Texas regulatory notice shows

A March 2026 Texas Register enforcement notice identified Wholesale Kings LLC’s Austin facility as a “kratom extract manufacturing plant” and listed a $26,796 Texas Commission on Environmental Quality penalty.

This is unusually direct public evidence linking a workbook consignee to domestic kratom processing. The notice concerns an environmental enforcement matter; it does not establish FDA compliance, product safety, customs intent, or the destination of any specific shipment.

Texas Register notice →

Documentary tension: Some shipment descriptions included dye/color language, while a state regulatory notice describes the facility as a kratom extract manufacturing plant. The records do not prove that the same cargo became extract, but the contrast warrants examination of entry documents, invoices, inventory records, and downstream manufacturing records.

Public Information Is Uneven

Some entities look like logistics companies

Names such as Via Express Cargo, Krown Logistics, and International Packing Solutions may indicate logistics or packaging functions. The name alone does not prove the entity’s precise role on a particular entry.

Some records point toward processing

The clearest example found is Wholesale Kings, which a Texas regulatory notice expressly described as operating a kratom extract manufacturing plant.

Some entities have little public-facing material

For several major consignees, shipment databases provide far more information than company websites or marketing materials. Absence of a public website does not mean absence of commercial activity.

What the Importer Ranking Does Not Prove

The ranking does not prove that the named consignee manufactured retail kratom, owned the cargo beneficially, acted as importer of record, or sold the material to consumers.

It shows which normalized U.S. entity names appeared in the curated public transport records, how much weight was associated with those records, and which supplier relationships repeated.

How the Company Profiles Were Built

  1. Workbook evidence: normalized consignee names, curated record counts, shipment weight, suppliers, ports, HS-code mix, wording flags, and arrival range.
  2. Government sources: official state corporate records and public regulatory notices.
  3. Commercial trade sources: used only to corroborate addresses, supplier relationships, and sample cargo wording where an underlying public manifest was described.
  4. Company-authored material: included only when a clearly attributable public website or statement could be located.
Limit: This page does not determine importer-of-record status, beneficial ownership, customs admissibility, FDA review, or the ultimate retail disposition of any shipment. Those questions require CBP entries, commercial invoices, broker records, FDA entry data, warehouse records, and downstream distribution documents.
