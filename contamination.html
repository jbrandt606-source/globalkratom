Product Contamination & Manufacturing Concerns

FDA recalls, laboratory analyses, and public-health investigations documenting contamination, adulteration, microbial hazards, and toxic metal exposure in commercial kratom products.

Key findings

  • Laboratory testing has identified disease-causing bacteria and toxic metals in commercial kratom products.
  • FDA recalls have included finished kratom powders and capsules.
  • Recent studies found missing or undisclosed ingredients and inaccurate potency labels.
  • Testing of some liquid products has identified substantial alcohol content.
CDC · November 26, 2026

Fatal exposure involving a product labeled as cat’s claw

A San Francisco death involved MGM-15, a semisynthetic opioid derived from kratom. Investigators detected MGM-15 in blood and in residue from the product packaging. No cat’s claw was detected in either sample. Other drugs were also detected in blood. The product was advertised as third-party tested, and its label did not list kratom or 7-OH.

Toxic metals in commercial products

FDA tested 30 kratom products and identified lead and nickel at concentrations that can lead to excessive daily intake. Long-term exposure can damage the nervous system and kidneys.
