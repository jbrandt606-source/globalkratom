Cardiac Toxicity & Arrhythmia Risk

Clinical reports and toxicologic literature documenting arrhythmias, conduction abnormalities, cardiac arrest, and severe cardiovascular events associated with kratom and mitragynine exposure.

Key Findings

  • Published reports document ventricular fibrillation, Brugada‑pattern ECG changes, QT prolongation, and sudden cardiac arrest.
  • Several cases required ICU‑level care, defibrillation, vasopressors, or implantable cardiac devices.
  • Toxicologic literature suggests mitragynine may affect cardiac ion channels (hERG, sodium channels) and electrophysiologic stability.
  • Severe cardiovascular outcomes have been reported in both polysubstance and isolated‑exposure settings.
  • Existing surveillance systems may underestimate cardiovascular involvement due to inconsistent toxicologic testing.

Clinical Implications

Healthcare providers, emergency physicians, and toxicologists should be aware of the potential for kratom‑associated cardiac instability, including:

  • Unexplained syncope or collapse – consider kratom use in differential
  • ECG changes (QT prolongation, Brugada pattern) without alternative cause
  • Ventricular arrhythmias or cardiac arrest, especially in young adults
  • Concurrent stimulant use (caffeine, amphetamines, cocaine) may potentiate risk
  • Perioperative planning should account for possible electrophysiologic effects
  • Routine toxicology screens do not detect kratom – clinical history essential

Published case reports and toxicologic studies increasingly associate kratom exposure with clinically significant electrophysiologic and cardiovascular instability, warranting consideration in emergency and cardiology settings.
